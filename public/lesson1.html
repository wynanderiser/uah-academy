Beginner tier · Lesson 1: One Object, One Light

Show me your egg, apple, or mug.

Before you begin

Today we will be drawing a simple object. Objects are never simple when we look closely. It might be an egg or a cup or a piece of fruit, but they all have their own texture and the way their texture plays with light. The cup reflects because it is glazed and glossy. The egg absorbs light with its matt surface. This lesson asks us to look closely, and use our chosen medium to practise putting down marks.

How light creates the illusion of form on a flat page, and getting basic proportion right. That's it — two things, nothing more.

What you'll need
  • One simple object: an egg, an apple, a mug, a pear — anything roughly round or oval
  • Whatever's nearest to hand to draw with — pencil, pen, biro on the back of an envelope is genuinely fine
  • A spot near a window with clear light coming from one side (not overhead light — you need a visible light side and a shadow side)
The exercise (15–20 minutes)
  1. Shape first. Get the outline roughly right before worrying about anything else.
  2. Find the light. Look for where it's brightest and where the shadow is darkest, and everything in between. Try to get that onto the page.
  3. Keep it centred. Don't overthink composition — just keep the object roughly in the middle so there's one clear thing to look at.

That's the whole brief. Don't aim for "good" — aim for honest.

Submitting it

Photograph it in natural daylight if you can, straight-on rather than at an angle, on a plain surface. Our AI teacher will provide feedback and offer suggestions. You can ask for more detail for a deeper analysis, and ask questions to clear up anything that's still confusing.
