Advanced tier · Advanced Lesson 2: Larger, Ambitious Pieces

Go bigger than feels safe.

Before you begin

Everything you've built so far has mostly lived on a small page, over a short sitting. Scale changes things. A bigger piece, or a more complex one, tests whether what you've learned actually holds — or whether it only worked because the last few lessons stayed small and forgiving.

Whether quality holds evenly across a genuinely larger or more complex piece, and whether one clear focal point survives the added complexity.

What you'll need
  • A subject with real scope — more objects, a bigger scene, a larger sheet of paper, more time than you've been giving a piece so far
  • Patience. This will likely take longer than anything else in the course so far, and that's the point
The exercise
  1. Choose something bigger than your instinct. If in doubt, go one size up from what feels comfortable.
  2. Work the whole piece, not just the easiest parts. It's tempting to over-focus on one favourite corner and let the rest go quiet — resist that.
  3. Step back often. On a larger piece, it's easy to lose the whole while working closely on one small part.

This lesson isn't measuring ambition for its own sake — it's checking whether everything else you've learned still holds at a larger scale.

Submitting it

Photograph it in natural daylight if you can, straight-on rather than at an angle, on a plain surface. Our AI teacher will provide feedback and offer suggestions. You can ask for more detail for a deeper analysis, and ask questions to clear up anything that's still confusing.
